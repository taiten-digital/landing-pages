# Brand DNA — Talita Lopes

> This is a rebuild of an existing live page, not a repositioning. This
> document describes what the brand already is across its official channels
> so the rebuild stays recognizable while improving execution. It does not
> propose new brand attributes.

## Confidence note on visual claims
Visual DNA below (color/typography/photography) is extracted via text-based
site fetches (markdown conversion + AI summary), not direct pixel/CSS
inspection or a rendered screenshot. Text content (headlines, CTAs, credential
language) is high-confidence/verbatim. Color hex values, exact typefaces, and
precise photography specifics are **lower-confidence, directional** — worth
confirming against a rendered screenshot or the site's source CSS before
locking exact hex codes/fonts in the rebuild.

## 1. Visual DNA

- **Color:** Predominantly white/light-neutral backgrounds with dark
  (black/gray) text and an unspecified accent tone — no bold or saturated
  brand color observed on either site. Reads as a clean, clinical-adjacent
  palette rather than a high-energy gym palette (no neon, no heavy reds/
  oranges typical of generic fitness marketing).
- **Typography:** Clean sans-serif throughout, moderate/professional weight,
  with mixed weights for hierarchy. The v1 page uses italic accents for
  emphasis (e.g., "Não o contrário." in italics against the roof headline) —
  a soft, conversational emphasis device rather than all-caps or exclamation-
  heavy styling.
- **Photography:** This is where the two properties diverge most.
  - Official site: mostly icon-based (fitness equipment icons, emblem-style
    graphics), minimal actual photography of Talita or clients.
  - v1 page: real lifestyle/instructional photography — outdoor walking,
    flexibility/mobility work, trainer-client interaction, with mature (50+)
    women visibly represented as subjects in active, non-studio settings.
  - This is a meaningful gap: the v1 page's photography does the work of
    showing the stated 50+/spinal-health positioning; the official site's
    icon-driven approach does not visually reinforce that niche at all.
- **Layout/density:** Moderate density, generous whitespace, grid-based
  sections. The v1 page is more structured as a conversion page: numbered
  "how it works" flow, dedicated testimonial section, FAQ accordion. The
  official site reads more like a general brochure site (about, services,
  contact) without the same funnel scaffolding.
- **Iconography:** Present on the official site for services/differentiators;
  arrows (➡️) used as a bullet/list device in the Instagram bio — a small,
  casual, recognizable tic worth preserving if reused in the rebuild's own
  copy (e.g., bio-style microcopy), but not something to over-extend into
  the main page's visual system.

## 2. Verbal DNA

- **Headline register:** Benefit-forward, second person, occasionally
  rhetorical-question-based. Examples verbatim:
  - Official site: "Resultados que fazem a diferença, com atendimento
    especializado"; "Cuide do seu corpo com quem entende de saúde!"; "Já
    pensou em alcançar seus objetivos com treinos personalizados e sem
    desculpas?"
  - v1 page: "Seu treino precisa se adaptar a você. _Não o contrário._";
    "Isso soa familiar?"
- **Sentence structure:** Short declarative or single-clause headlines;
  body copy in plain, unadorned Portuguese sentences — no jargon, no
  technical exercise-science vocabulary surfaced in any consumer-facing copy
  reviewed (credentials are stated plainly: "Formada em Educação Física pela
  UNOPAR," "Especialista em exercício físico para distúrbios da coluna
  vertebral").
- **Formality level:** Mid-formal — polite "você" address (not "tu", not
  gírias), professional but not stiff/corporate. Consistent with a regulated
  health-adjacent profession speaking to an 50+-inclusive audience: no slang,
  no heavy internet-fitness-bro vocabulary ("shape," "quebrar o corpo," etc.
  do not appear), no excessive exclamation points despite one exclamation-
  point headline on the official site.
- **Section-title convention (v1 page only):** Short, plain-Portuguese
  labels — "Sobre a Talita," "Como funciona," "Por que treinar com a
  Talita," "Serviços," "O que dizem os alunos," "Perguntas frequentes,"
  "Fale com a Talita." This is a clear, reusable naming convention for the
  rebuild's information architecture.
- **Credential language:** Always stated as fact, not marketing flourish —
  "CREF 019973-G/PR," "Formada em Educação Física pela UNOPAR," "+10 anos de
  experiência." No embellishment ("top," "premiada," "referência") layered on
  top of these; the brand lets the credentials speak plainly. This restraint
  itself is part of the verbal DNA and should be preserved — do not
  "punch up" credential copy with superlatives not already present.
- **Instagram bio register is notably terser/more functional** than either
  site: "Talita Lopes | Personal Trainer / #personaltrainer / CREF
  019973-G/PR / ➡️Londrina/Pr / ➡️Avaliação Física." Almost telegraphic —
  credential, location, one CTA-adjacent line, arrows as bullets. Feed
  content is described as educational/motivational (training philosophy
  quotes, back-pain and strength-training education posts), which is
  consistent in substance with the site's spine/health specialization angle,
  even though the bio itself reads more clipped than the sites' prose.
- **Words the brand avoids (observed, not stated):** No hard-sell urgency
  language ("última vaga," "oferta por tempo limitado"), no guarantee/
  money-back language, no medical-diagnostic claims beyond "especialista em
  exercício físico para distúrbios da coluna vertebral" (an exercise
  specialization claim, not a treatment/cure claim) — this line should be
  preserved carefully as-is rather than strengthened into anything that
  reads as a medical promise, given the regulated-profession context.

## 3. Commercial DNA

- **Core promise:** Personalized, adaptive training from a credentialed
  specialist, for people (frequently 50+, frequently with spinal/back
  concerns) who have not gotten results elsewhere. The v1 page's tagline
  states the mechanism plainly: training adapts to the person, not the
  reverse.
- **Main argument/mechanism:** Individualization + specialization
  (spinal-column exercise focus) as the differentiator against generic
  personal training — not price, not gym amenities, not group-class energy.
- **Proof style:** Credential-led (CREF number, degree, years of experience)
  plus, on the v1 page only, named-outcome testimonials explicitly labeled
  "Verified Google review" and a "5 stars / 28 reviews" claim. Proof is
  presented plainly/quietly — no video testimonials, no before/after photos,
  no influencer-style endorsement format.
- **Objection handling:** Implicit rather than explicit — the v1 page's "Isso
  soa familiar?" section and testimonial about "over two years with other
  professionals without results" pre-empt the "I've already tried and it
  didn't work" objection, but there is no dedicated objection-handling copy
  block (e.g., no explicit "but is online consulting as effective?" FAQ
  answer confirmed in this pass — worth checking during copywriting).
- **Offer language:** Free physical assessment ("avaliação física" /
  "Marcar avaliação") as the singular funnel entry point, converted via
  WhatsApp only. No pricing, packages, or self-serve checkout on any channel
  — this absence is a consistent, apparently deliberate pattern across every
  channel checked, not a gap unique to one property.
- **CTA convention:** "Fale com a Talita" (v1 page section title) / "Marcar
  avaliação" — first-name-first, personal-relationship framing rather than
  generic "Agende agora" or "Compre já" transactional framing.

## 4. Personality spectrum placement

- **Formal ↔ Casual:** Mid-leaning-formal. Polite, credential-forward,
  "você" address; not casual/slangy, not corporate-stiff either.
- **Playful ↔ Serious:** Serious-leaning, with warmth. No humor, no jokes,
  no playful visual devices beyond the small arrow bullets in the Instagram
  bio. Matches a health-adjacent, credentialed-professional register.
- **Premium ↔ Accessible:** Accessible/approachable, not luxury-positioned.
  No pricing shown (which could read as premium-exclusive in some
  categories), but the tone, plain language, and absence of luxury visual
  cues (no gold accents, no "elite"/"exclusivo" language found) point to
  accessible-professional rather than premium/boutique-luxury.
- **Clinical ↔ Warm:** Sits in between and pulls the two properties in
  different directions — the official site's icon-driven, testimonial-free
  presentation reads more clinical/brochure-like; the v1 page's real
  photography, testimonials, and "Isso soa familiar?" framing reads
  noticeably warmer and more emotionally attuned to the 50+/pain-relief
  audience. The rebuild should lean toward the v1 page's warmer register,
  since it's the version carrying the audience-specific proof and imagery.

## 5. Inconsistencies to flag (not smoothed over)

1. **Testimonials/social proof exist on only one property.** The official
   site (talitalopespersonal.com.br) shows zero testimonials; the v1 landing
   page carries all three named-outcome Google-review quotes plus the
   "5 stars / 28 reviews" claim. If the rebuild treats the official site as
   ground truth for "what the brand looks like," it will lose the strongest
   proof asset. Recommend treating the v1 page as the source of record for
   proof content, while confirming the 28-review count is still current
   before reusing it (per company-intelligence.md).
2. **Photography approach differs sharply between properties.** Official
   site is icon/graphic-led with little real photography; v1 page uses real,
   demographically-relevant lifestyle photography (mature subjects, active
   settings). These read as two different visual systems, not one brand
   extended across two pages. The rebuild should pick one (the v1 approach
   is the stronger fit for the stated 50+/spinal-health niche) rather than
   blending both inconsistently.
3. **Official site's headline energy is slightly more generic-fitness-marketing**
   ("...e sem desculpas?" — "no excuses" framing is a common generic-gym
   trope) **than the v1 page's**, which speaks more specifically and gently
   to the "tried everything else, nothing worked" pain point. This is a
   tonal seam: one line ("no excuses") reads mildly at odds with the
   otherwise patient, "ouve e adapta o treino" (listens and adapts)
   positioning aimed at a 50+/pain-affected audience. Flagging rather than
   carrying it forward silently — recommend the rebuild follow the v1 page's
   gentler, listens-and-adapts register over the "no excuses" framing.
4. **Instagram bio is much terser than either site's prose**, functioning as
   a directory/credential card rather than brand voice in full. This is
   normal for an Instagram bio's format constraints and is not treated here
   as a contradiction requiring resolution — just noted as a different
   register that shouldn't be mistaken for the brand's core verbal style.
5. **A second Instagram handle (@talitaapersonal) exists with no confirmed
   connection to this brand** (no shared CREF, location, or cross-links per
   company-intelligence.md). It is out of scope for brand DNA (not confirmed
   as the same professional) but flagged here so no visual/verbal cues are
   accidentally pulled from that account during the rebuild.
6. **Gerontology specialization is referenced in prior discovery notes but
   not confirmed verbatim on any primary source fetched directly** (see
   company-intelligence.md). Treat as an open credential-wording question,
   not as confirmed verbal DNA — do not originate new phrasing for it here.

## 6. Do / Don't guidance for the rebuild

**Do:**
- Keep the plain, credential-first proof style (CREF, degree, years) stated
  factually without superlatives.
- Keep the polite, mid-formal "você" register — no slang, no gym-bro
  vocabulary, no excessive exclamation points.
- Keep the v1 page's section-naming convention ("Sobre a Talita," "Como
  funciona," "O que dizem os alunos," etc.) as it's already a clear, working
  IA.
- Keep WhatsApp as the sole conversion path and "avaliação física" as the
  singular free-entry offer; do not introduce pricing or self-serve checkout
  without explicit client sign-off (pricing appears to be a deliberate
  omission across every channel).
- Keep real, demographically-relevant photography (mature adults in active,
  non-studio settings) as the visual anchor for the 50+/spinal-health
  positioning — this is the strongest existing visual asset and the most
  underused one (official site barely uses it).
- Keep the exercise-specialization framing ("especialista em exercício
  físico para distúrbios da coluna vertebral") worded exactly as a
  fitness/exercise specialization, not a medical/treatment claim.

**Don't:**
- Don't adopt hard-sell, urgency, or generic-gym tropes ("sem desculpas,"
  countdown/scarcity language, all-caps CTAs) — inconsistent with the
  brand's otherwise patient, listens-and-adapts tone toward its core 50+
  audience.
- Don't invent guarantee or money-back language — none exists on any channel.
- Don't strengthen the spinal-health claim into anything reading as a
  medical/diagnostic promise; this is a regulated-profession compliance risk
  flagged in company-intelligence.md.
- Don't carry over the official site's icon-only visual approach as the
  primary look — it under-represents the audience the brand actually serves
  compared to the v1 page's photography.
- Don't state the gerontology specialization or the "28 verified reviews"
  count as confirmed facts without client re-confirmation (both are flagged
  open items in company-intelligence.md).
- Don't reference or draw visual/verbal cues from @talitaapersonal — it is
  not confirmed as the same brand.
