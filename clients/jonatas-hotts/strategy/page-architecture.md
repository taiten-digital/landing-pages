## Final Section Order
1. **Hero**
2. **Who it's for / offer clarity** (in-person vs. online, explained)
3. **What training with him is like** (tone + demonstration content)
4. **How it starts** (assessment-first process)
5. **Contact / final CTA** (+ minimal faith-line aside)

Five sections, matching strategy's own length call. The audit above
confirms that call rather than assuming it: every rejected candidate either
has no verified content to put in it (testimonials, pricing, certifications,
extended bio) or would restate an objection a kept section already owns
(FAQ, a separate differentiators block). There was no section trimmed from
strategy's proposed shape and none added back — five holds.

## User Journey Walkthrough
1. **Arrival (Hero).** A visitor — cold from search/ads or warm from
   Instagram — lands and within one glance gets: who Jonatas is, that he
   trains people in Londrina in person or online, and a visible WhatsApp CTA.
   Nobody has to scroll to find the exit action, even if they don't take it
   yet.
2. **"Is this actually for me?" (Offer clarity).** The visitor who scrolls
   past the hero without tapping is doing so because they need more context,
   not because the CTA was hidden. This section answers the two questions
   strategy identifies as the real early hesitation — general-fitness fit,
   and which delivery format applies to them — in plain terms neither
   Jonatas's own channels nor any local competitor currently offers.
3. **"Can I trust this guy?" (What it's like).** Having self-identified as
   the right audience, the visitor's next real doubt is legitimacy, and this
   page has no testimonial or certification to hand them. Tone and (if
   sourced) real demonstration footage do that job instead — this is where
   the page either earns the tap or doesn't, since it's the last persuasion
   beat before the ask.
4. **"What actually happens if I message him?" (How it starts).** Right
   before the final CTA, the page defuses the specific friction of messaging
   a stranger by naming the first step as a free/low-stakes assessment
   conversation, not a sales pitch or a commitment.
5. **Convert (Contact / final CTA).** The visitor who has now cleared fit,
   trust, and friction concerns meets the CTA a final time with nothing left
   to resolve. The faith-line aside sits here, small and non-blocking, so a
   visitor who cares about it can notice it without it ever standing between
   anyone and the WhatsApp tap.

CTA placement: repeats at Hero, at the close of "How it starts," and again
in "Contact / final CTA" — three exposures at natural breaks, matching
strategy's explicit instruction (repetition, not a second distinct action,
removes doubt about how to convert).

## Per-Section Notes

### Hero
- **What it communicates:** Who Jonatas is, the core promise ("a trainer
  who meets you where you are — in person around Londrina or online"), and
  an immediately visible primary CTA.
- **What happens if removed:** Impossible to remove — every visitor's first
  impression happens here; without it the page has no orientation and loses
  its first (and for a quick scroller, possibly only) CTA exposure.

### Who it's for / offer clarity
- **What it communicates:** The general-fitness/everyday-goals audience
  stated plainly, and a clear, concrete explanation of what the in-person
  option includes versus what the online option includes — not just that
  both exist.
- **What happens if removed:** The page's single biggest differentiation
  lever disappears. A visitor is left to guess whether they're the right
  fit and which format suits them — exactly the unmet gap both research
  docs identify as currently open across Jonatas's own channels and every
  local competitor.

### What training with him is like
- **What it communicates:** Voice-led trust: casual, direct,
  demonstration-led coaching style, ideally backed by real photo/video of
  Jonatas actually training people (per visual-research). Functions as this
  page's substitute for a testimonials section it can't honestly run.
- **What happens if removed:** The "is this legit / is he qualified"
  objection goes completely unaddressed — strategy names this as the one
  objection the page currently can't answer with hard proof, so removing
  the one section built to answer it indirectly leaves it fully open.

### How it starts
- **What it communicates:** The first message leads to a real, low-stakes
  assessment conversation, not a hard sell — directly reduces the
  psychological cost of messaging a stranger, which strategy identifies as
  the real barrier (not skepticism about personal training as a category).
- **What happens if removed:** The CTA loses its strongest piece of
  friction-reduction right before the ask; a hesitant visitor has one less
  reason to believe tapping WhatsApp is safe.

### Contact / final CTA
- **What it communicates:** The final, unambiguous WhatsApp CTA, restated
  after every objection has had a chance to be addressed, plus the
  faith-line aside (small, present, non-prominent — a short line, not a
  headline or repeated motif, per brand-dna.md's explicit judgment).
- **What happens if removed:** There's no terminal conversion moment and no
  sanctioned place left for the faith-line — it would either get dropped
  entirely (understating something Jonatas chose to state himself) or get
  awkwardly inserted elsewhere at a weight it hasn't earned.
