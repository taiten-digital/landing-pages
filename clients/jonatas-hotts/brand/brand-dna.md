## 1. Visual DNA

- **Photography style:** Candid, real gym/workout footage — his own
  training/coaching clips, not staged studio photography or stock imagery.
  Content mixes weight-training demonstrations, cardio-equipment use, and
  short technique-correction clips (squat pad positioning, elbow form,
  treadmill form). This reads as an *authority-through-demonstration*
  visual approach rather than a posed-portrait or lifestyle-photography
  approach — closer to "here's proper form, watch" than "here's an
  aspirational trainer photo."
- **Format:** Meme-format / text-overlay video clips are a recurring
  device — Portuguese text overlays calling out a specific form cue or
  common mistake, built for fast social consumption and shareability, not
  polished long-form content.
- **Color/typography:** Not independently verifiable from the sources
  available (see confidence note above) — no hex values, brand color, or
  specific typeface can be asserted as fact. Flag for the visual-research/
  ui-design phases to gather directly from exported posts or a live view
  rather than assume a "typical gym" palette (reds/blacks/neon) without
  evidence.
- **Energy:** Instructional and energetic, but informal — closer to "a guy
  who knows what he's doing and will show you on camera" than a polished,
  branded-content operation. No evidence of a designed visual system
  (consistent color grading, logo lockup, template graphics) across the
  content described — this looks like an individual practitioner's organic
  content, not an agency-managed brand.

## 2. Verbal DNA

- **Bio (verbatim, both fetches agreed):** "Jonatas Hotts | Seu Personal em
  Londrina 🎯🏋️‍♀️ Consultoria Online que funciona Jesus é o caminho ✝️"
- **Register:** Short, punchy, directory-card-like — role, location, one
  efficacy claim ("que funciona" — "that works," stated plainly with no
  attached proof), then the faith line. Emoji used functionally as visual
  bullets/markers (🎯🏋️‍♀️✝️), not decoratively throughout — consistent
  with an Instagram-bio format constraint rather than a broader "emoji-
  heavy" voice; don't over-extend this into full-page copy.
- **Post captions/content themes:** Plain, colloquial Portuguese framed as
  direct questions or common-complaint hooks — "Enjoei do exercício, e
  agora?" ("Bored with exercise, now what?"). This is conversational,
  second-person-adjacent, low-formality language, not clinical or
  credential-heavy phrasing. No jargon or exercise-science vocabulary
  observed in caption themes.
- **Formality level:** Casual-leaning. "Approachable humor through meme
  formatting" (WebFetch's own characterization) plus informal caption
  hooks put this closer to *casual* than *mid-formal* on the spectrum —
  a clear contrast with this project's prior client (Talita Lopes), whose
  documented brand DNA is mid-formal/credential-led. Don't default to the
  same register for Jonatas; his own content is looser and more
  demonstration/humor-driven.
- **Words/patterns present:** Direct address to a common frustration
  ("enjoei," "bored"), technique-correction framing, one bare efficacy
  claim ("que funciona") with no elaboration or proof attached.
- **Words/patterns not observed:** No credential language surfaced
  anywhere in his own bio or captions (no CREF number stated — see
  company-intelligence.md, this is a PROOF NEEDED gap, not a stylistic
  choice to avoid it). No pricing language. No testimonial/review language.
  No superlatives ("o melhor," "referência," "premiado").

## 3. Commercial DNA

- **Core promise (his own words):** Online consulting "que funciona" —
  a bare efficacy claim, no mechanism or proof stated publicly.
- **Offer structure:** Dual — in-person (home visits, condo-gym,
  commercial-gym sessions per GuiaFix) and online consulting, both
  mentioned but not differentiated anywhere public (who each is for, what's
  included). This is a real gap in his current public presence, not a
  brand trait to preserve — the landing page is a legitimate place to make
  this legible for the first time.
- **Proof style:** None publicly present. No certifications displayed
  (CREF near-certainly exists as a legal requirement but is unconfirmed —
  do not state or imply it without client confirmation), no testimonials,
  no client counts, no before/afters. **This absence of proof is itself
  part of the current brand reality** — the landing page starts from zero
  public proof, not from an under-surfaced proof stack that just needs
  restating.
- **Pricing:** Not stated anywhere, consistent with the local competitive
  set (per company-intelligence.md, other Londrina trainer listings also
  omit pricing) — this reads as category-normal, not a Jonatas-specific
  gap to fix.
- **CTA convention:** WhatsApp-first (already the client's stated CTA
  design per project-state.md — "agendar avaliação"), consistent with
  his own bio/Linktree routing everything to direct contact rather than
  a booking system or price list.

## 4. Positioning signal — general fitness coach, not elite/competition coach

**Confirmed, not challenged.** The evidence converges from multiple
independent angles:
- Content themes are technique/form correction and everyday motivation
  ("bored of exercise, now what?"), not physique-competition prep,
  before/after transformation posting, or elite-athlete content.
- GuiaFix's third-party description of his in-person offer explicitly
  frames it as health, conditioning, weight loss, and muscle gain — a
  general-population framing.
- Follower count (~1,505) and content format (candid demonstration clips,
  meme-format captions) read as a working local trainer's organic
  content, not an influencer-scale or competition-circuit operation.
- Nothing in his bio, captions, or third-party listings references
  competition prep, physique judging, or an elite-athlete clientele.

**Implication for strategy/copy:** position him as an accessible,
everyday-people coach (weight loss, general conditioning, "get back into
training") — not a physique/performance specialist. This should directly
inform the strategist's audience definition next.

## 5. The faith/religious line — explicit call

**Judgment: treat as a personal-identity aside, not a core brand pillar —
keep present but not prominent on the landing page.** Reasoning:

- It appears exactly once, as the closing line of an Instagram bio that is
  otherwise entirely functional (name, role, location, offer). It is not
  repeated in the Linktree, not referenced in any post caption theme
  observed, and not elaborated anywhere. A single unexpanded line in a
  bio format that's mostly used for identity-card facts reads as *personal
  disclosure*, not a stated brand pillar or ministry-adjacent positioning
  (contrast with, e.g., an explicitly "faith-based fitness coaching" brand,
  which would thread it through captions, offer language, and visuals
  repeatedly — none of that pattern is present here).
- The rest of his public content (technique clips, general motivation,
  GuiaFix's generic health/conditioning framing) gives no indication his
  service is positioned *as* faith-based coaching — the offer and audience
  signals are entirely secular/generic-fitness.
- However, it is real and stated in his own words, not assumed — removing
  it entirely from the landing page would understate something he chose
  to put in his primary bio. The right move is a small, tasteful,
  non-load-bearing acknowledgment (e.g., a footer line or an "about him"
  aside) rather than a hero headline, campaign theme, or repeated motif.
- **This is a judgment call from available evidence, not a client
  confirmation** — company-intelligence.md already flags this as an open
  discovery-questionnaire item. If the client is reachable before the
  strategy phase locks, confirm directly rather than relying solely on
  this inference; until then, strategy/copy should treat "present, minimal,
  tasteful" as the working default rather than leaving it unresolved.

## 6. Personality spectrum placement

- **Formal ↔ Casual:** Casual-leaning. Colloquial captions, meme-format
  content, no credential-forward language.
- **Playful ↔ Serious:** Leans playful/approachable within a serious
  subject (proper exercise form) — "approachable humor through meme
  formatting" is the clearest available descriptor.
- **Premium ↔ Accessible:** Accessible. No pricing shown, no luxury/
  boutique cues, general-population offer framing (weight loss,
  conditioning) rather than elite/specialist positioning.
- **Polished-brand ↔ Individual-practitioner:** Sits clearly on the
  individual-practitioner end — no evidence of a designed visual system,
  consistent color grading, or agency-level content production. The
  landing page can be the first place this brand gets a deliberate visual
  system, but should still feel like *him* (demonstration-led, informal,
  direct) rather than a generic polished-studio look grafted on top.

## 7. Inconsistencies / gaps to flag (not smoothed over)

1. **Dual offer (in-person + online) is unexplained anywhere public.**
   Both are mentioned, neither is differentiated. Not a brand
   inconsistency to preserve — a genuine information gap for ux-architect/
   copywriter to close.
2. **Zero public proof stack** (no certification shown, no testimonials,
   no client counts) in a local market where at least one direct
   competitor (per company-intelligence.md) does publicly cite a CREF
   number. This is a real competitive gap, not a stylistic choice — do
   not manufacture proof to close it; if the client can supply CREF number
   or real testimonials privately, that's a legitimate differentiator to
   surface later.
3. **The bare efficacy claim "que funciona"** ("that works") has no
   attached mechanism or evidence anywhere public. Keep this kind of claim
   only if softened/contextualized in copy (e.g., paired with what the
   actual approach is), not amplified into a stronger unproven claim.
4. **No visual system currently exists** to extend — unlike Talita Lopes
   (which had two properties with an established, if inconsistent, visual
   language to reconcile), Jonatas's brand DNA here is almost entirely
   verbal/positional. Visual direction will be built fresh from tone (casual,
   demonstration-led, accessible) rather than extracted from an existing
   palette/type system. Flag this explicitly to ui-design so it isn't
   mistaken for a research gap rather than the actual state of the brand.
5. **Follower count and bio text come from a single access method**
   (WebFetch, no logged-in browser) across two fetches that agreed with
   each other — moderate, not high, confidence per company-intelligence.md.
   Worth a manual spot-check before any of this is treated as locked fact
   in copy.

## 8. Do / Don't guidance

**Do:**
- Keep the voice casual, direct, and demonstration-led — someone who
  shows proper form on camera and talks to common frustrations
  ("enjoei do exercício") rather than a credential-first, polished-brand
  voice.
- Position him as a general fitness/conditioning/weight-loss coach for
  everyday people — not an elite or competition-prep specialist.
- Keep the faith line present but minimal — a small, tasteful mention
  (e.g., footer or short "about" aside), not a headline or repeated theme.
- Route everything to WhatsApp as the single conversion path, consistent
  with his existing bio/Linktree behavior.
- Explain the in-person vs. online offer clearly on the page — this is a
  real, currently-unmet need, not a brand trait to leave ambiguous.
- Mark any certification, pricing, or testimonial as PROOF NEEDED rather
  than asserting it; none of these are currently verifiable.

**Don't:**
- Don't default to Talita Lopes's mid-formal, credential-led register —
  the two brands are verifiably different in formality and proof-posture;
  copying that pattern here would misrepresent this brand.
- Don't state or imply a CREF number, certification, testimonial, review
  count, or client outcome that hasn't been confirmed by the client.
- Don't expand the faith line into a headline, campaign theme, or
  repeated motif without explicit client direction — the evidence
  available supports "present" but not "central."
- Don't invent a color palette or typeface and present it as derived from
  his existing brand — none is verifiable yet; ui-design should build
  fresh from tone, or request real visual references from the client.
- Don't position him as a physique/competition/elite-athlete coach — no
  evidence supports that niche, and the general-fitness positioning is
  well-corroborated.
