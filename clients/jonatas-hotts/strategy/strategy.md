# Strategy — Jonatas Hotts

## Objective
Get a visitor to open WhatsApp and message Jonatas to "agendar uma
avaliação" (book a trial assessment). That's the only conversion event this
page has — there's no price list, no booking calendar, no account to
create. Traffic is mixed organic/social (Instagram bio-link traffic most
likely, plus possible local paid ads) arriving with low-to-medium
sophistication about Jonatas specifically (some will already follow him on
Instagram and recognize his content/tone; others land cold from a search or
an ad and know nothing). Either way, nobody arrives comparison-shopping
features or price — Londrina's entire local personal-trainer category
routes to WhatsApp with no public pricing (competitor-analysis.md), so a
visitor already expects "message to find out more," not "buy on this page."
The job of the page is narrower than "sell training": it has to give a cold
or lukewarm visitor enough clarity and trust in about one screen's worth of
attention to make the WhatsApp tap feel like a safe, obvious next step, not
a leap of faith.

## Audience & Traffic Context
- **Who:** everyday people in Londrina, PR wanting general fitness —
  weight loss, conditioning, "get back into training" — not athletes,
  not physique-competition clients. This is confirmed by brand-dna.md
  (content themes, GuiaFix's own description of his offer) and is not up
  for reinterpretation at the strategy stage.
- **Two sub-segments by delivery preference**, both real per
  company-intelligence.md: people who want someone physically present
  (home, condo gym, or a commercial gym session) and people who want
  online coaching and don't need him in the room. The page has to serve
  both without forcing a visitor to guess which one they are before they
  even know the offer exists — see Messaging Hierarchy.
- **Traffic source:** not instrumented or specified by the client
  (questionnaire.md) — treat as mixed organic social + possible local
  paid. This matters for length: paid local-service traffic in particular
  tends to be impatient and comparison-averse (they want the answer, then
  the WhatsApp tap), which supports keeping the page short rather than
  assuming a long nurture sequence is needed.
- **Prior knowledge:** assume low. Even Instagram followers who click
  through don't currently get an explanation anywhere (his own channels)
  of what in-person vs. online actually includes — brand-dna.md flags
  this as a real, unmet gap, not something the page can lean on being
  already known.

## Offer
General personal training in Londrina, delivered either in-person (home
visits, condo-gym, or commercial-gym sessions, per the GuiaFix-sourced
description in company-intelligence.md) or online coaching/consulting.
Target outcomes: weight loss, general conditioning, muscle gain, "getting
back into training" — not sport-specific or competition prep. No verified
price, package structure, or session count exists (PROOF NEEDED, both
research docs) — the page cannot state a price and shouldn't gesture at
tiers it can't back up. The offer as the page can honestly state it is:
"train with Jonatas, in person or online, the way that fits your life" —
with the actual terms (price, frequency, format) left to the WhatsApp
conversation, matching the entire local category's own convention
(competitor-analysis.md: every Londrina competitor found does the same).

## Core Promise
"A personal trainer who meets you where you are — in person around
Londrina or online, for everyday goals, not gym-bro intimidation."

This leans on what's actually verifiable and differentiating right now:
dual delivery format explained clearly (a real, currently-unclaimed gap
per both research docs) and an accessible, non-elite positioning (verified
by brand-dna.md's content analysis) — not on unverifiable efficacy or
credential claims. It deliberately does not repeat the bare "que funciona"
claim from his Instagram bio at face value; if that phrase or a close
relative is used in copy, it should be paired with something concrete
(what the actual process looks like: assessment first, plan built around
you) rather than asserted alone, per brand-dna.md's explicit guidance not
to amplify an unproven bare claim.

## Messaging Hierarchy
1. **Who this is for and what you get** — general-fitness coaching (not
   elite/competition), available in-person around Londrina or online,
   explained clearly enough that a visitor immediately knows which one
   applies to them. This is the single highest-value thing the page can
   do that his own current channels don't (both research docs flag this
   as the clearest open gap) — it comes first because clarity of offer is
   this page's main lever, given there's no price or proof stack to lead
   with instead.
2. **What working with him actually feels like** — casual, direct,
   demonstration-led, real coaching from a real local trainer (not a
   templated app or a celebrity-scale online brand). This is where tone
   does the trust-building work that testimonials/certifications would
   normally do elsewhere — brand-dna.md's verbal DNA (plain colloquial
   Portuguese, technique-correction framing, no credential-forward
   language) is the actual asset here and should read through in the
   page's voice, not just be described.
3. **How to start** — book a free/initial assessment via WhatsApp. Low-
   commitment framing ("just a conversation to see what fits") reduces
   the friction of a first message to a stranger, which is the real
   psychological barrier this page has to clear, not "why should I trust
   personal training as a category."
4. **(If client confirms) a real proof element** — CREF number, a
   genuine testimonial, or a before/after, inserted only if and when
   verified. Ranked last deliberately: it's the strongest lever available
   *if* it becomes available, but nothing here can be asserted now, so
   the page's structure and hero-level persuasion must not depend on it
   existing.

## Differentiators
- **Dual in-person + online offer, explained rather than just mentioned.**
  Every local competitor with this combination (Talita Lopes, Vêneto, RP
  Fit, and Jonatas's own current channels) leaves it as an unexplained
  bio line. A page that plainly answers "what do I get with each option"
  is unclaimed locally (competitor-analysis.md, brand-dna.md).
- **Accessible, non-intimidating tone for everyday goals.** Verified by
  his own content (technique-correction clips, "bored of exercise, now
  what?" captions) — genuinely his voice, not a persona invented for the
  page. No local competitor in the researched set is documented using
  this register; it's a real point of difference, not just a style
  choice.
- **A named audience focus (general fitness/weight loss/conditioning),
  stated plainly.** Every local competitor with a clear niche (Vêneto's
  police-exam prep, Talita Lopes's 50+/spine focus) states it outright.
  Jonatas doesn't currently state his anywhere public even though his
  actual content already supports one. Making it explicit on the page
  (see Strategic Assumptions & Risks for the one open question this
  raises) costs nothing to state honestly and matches local convention.
- **What NOT to claim as a differentiator right now:** price transparency,
  review counts, before/afters, or credentials — none are verified
  (PROOF NEEDED across both research docs). Do not imply Jonatas is
  more credentialed or more reviewed than competitors; that would be
  fabrication, not differentiation.

## Proof to Use
Nothing quantifiable exists yet. Per this project's content-integrity
rule, the page cannot state a CREF number, certification, review count,
testimonial, or client count that hasn't been confirmed by the client —
all of these are explicitly flagged PROOF NEEDED in company-intelligence.md
and brand-dna.md. Where the instinct would normally be to add a stat or
quote, this page substitutes:
- **Demonstrated competence over asserted competence** — if real
  photo/video content from Jonatas's own training sessions is available
  (visual-research phase should pursue this), showing him actually
  coaching does more honest trust-building than a claimed credential,
  and matches his own existing content style (brand-dna.md: candid
  demonstration footage, not posed studio photography).
- **Process clarity as a trust signal** — spelling out that the first
  step is a real assessment (not a hard sell) does some of the job a
  guarantee or testimonial would do elsewhere, without inventing either.
- **A neutral, honest proof placeholder is acceptable where a stronger
  claim would be dishonest** — e.g. general "registered/qualified
  personal trainer" language only if and once the client confirms a CREF
  number; until then, omit the claim rather than imply it vaguely.
- If the client supplies a verified CREF number, real testimonial, or
  genuine before/after during copywriting, that becomes the strongest
  available proof element and should be added then — this strategy
  should not be read as saying proof is unwanted, only that none exists
  to plan around yet.

## Objections to Address
- **"Is this for someone like me, or for serious athletes?"** — answered
  directly by stating the general-fitness/everyday-goals positioning up
  front (Messaging Hierarchy #1), not left implicit.
- **"In-person or online — which do I even want, and what's the
  difference?"** — answered by explaining both formats in plain terms
  instead of leaving them as an unexplained bundle (the clearest gap
  identified in both research docs).
- **"Is this legit / is he actually qualified?"** — this is the one
  objection the page currently cannot fully answer with hard proof. Address
  it honestly through tone, transparency about the assessment-first
  process, and (if the client confirms) a real credential — not through
  vague implication. Do not paper over this gap with soft language that
  reads as if proof exists when it doesn't.
- **"What does it cost?"** — not answered on the page, matching
  category-wide local convention (no competitor publishes pricing
  either, per competitor-analysis.md). The page should say plainly that
  pricing/plan details are discussed on WhatsApp rather than staying
  silent on the topic and leaving visitors to wonder if that's an
  oversight.
- **"Messaging a stranger on WhatsApp feels like a commitment."** —
  addressed by framing the first contact as a low-stakes assessment
  conversation, not a sales call, consistent with the CTA wording the
  client already specified ("agendar avaliação").

## Primary CTA
WhatsApp — "Agendar avaliação" (schedule a trial assessment), per the
client's own stated CTA design. No secondary CTA is needed or justified:
there's no email list, no lead magnet, no booking calendar to route to
instead, and adding one would just split attention on a page whose entire
job is one action. The CTA should repeat at natural break points (hero,
after the offer explanation, end of page) rather than appear once, since
repetition — not a second distinct action — is what local convention and
this project's own prior client (Talita Lopes) rely on to remove any
doubt about how to convert.

## Page Length & Structure Rationale
**Short.** This is a single, low-complexity, zero-cost, reversible action
(sending a WhatsApp message), not a purchase, signup, or multi-step
commitment — the standard case for a long page (justifying a big price,
walking through many features/tiers, overcoming heavy skepticism with a
proof stack) doesn't apply here, and there's no proof stack to stack even
if length were otherwise justified. Local category convention reinforces
this: every researched Londrina competitor (Talita Lopes being the fullest
example, at hero → 3 pillars → about → 6 services → 4 differentiators →
contact) stays well short of the national online-coaching brands'
much longer, pricing-and-proof-laden pages (Treino Ninja, Consultoria
Personal) — because those brands are asking for a purchase decision on
the page itself, and Jonatas isn't.

Target shape, roughly five sections, all above or barely below one
"scroll session" of attention:
1. **Hero** — who he is, the core promise, the primary CTA immediately
   visible.
2. **Who this is for / what you get** — general-fitness positioning +
   in-person vs. online explained (Messaging Hierarchy #1), the section
   doing the most differentiation work on this page.
3. **What it's like to train with him** — tone/voice-led section, real
   demonstration content if available (Messaging Hierarchy #2), doubling
   as this page's substitute for a testimonials section it can't
   honestly include yet.
4. **How it starts** — plain description of the assessment-first process,
   pre-empting the "is messaging a commitment" objection, CTA repeated.
5. **Contact / final CTA** — WhatsApp, plus the tasteful, minimal faith-
   line aside per brand-dna.md's explicit judgment (present, not
   prominent — a footer/about aside, not a section of its own).

No pricing section, no testimonials section, no FAQ-as-padding, and no
separate certifications section unless the client supplies a verified
CREF number or credential during copywriting (in which case it earns a
single line, not a dedicated block, since one data point doesn't need its
own section). Adding sections to look more substantial than a five-section
page would be padding, not persuasion — it would work against, not for,
conversion on a single low-friction CTA.

## Strategic Assumptions & Risks
- **Stated niche is a strategy-level recommendation, not yet a client
  confirmation.** Both research docs treat "general fitness/conditioning/
  weight loss for everyday people" as a well-corroborated inference from
  his own content and GuiaFix's description, and this strategy relies on
  stating it plainly on the page (matching local convention). If the
  client actually wants to stay a broader generalist, or has a different
  self-image than the content suggests, this should be confirmed before
  copywriting locks the phrasing in.
- **The faith-identity line is carried forward per brand-dna.md's
  explicit judgment call (present, minimal, non-load-bearing) — not a
  confirmed client instruction.** If the client is reachable before
  copywriting, confirm directly; this strategy is not the place to
  resolve it and doesn't try to.
- **Zero verified proof (CREF, testimonials, before/afters) is the
  biggest real risk to conversion strength**, not a strategy oversight —
  it's the honest state of what's available today. This strategy
  compensates with tone, offer clarity, and process transparency rather
  than fabricating proof, per this project's content-integrity rule, but
  that's a real trade-off, not a solved problem: if the client can supply
  any one verified proof element before launch, it should be added
  (Proof to Use, item 4 in Messaging Hierarchy) rather than left out for
  consistency with this document.
- **Visual assets are unconfirmed.** Brand-dna.md notes no visual system
  exists to extract from and photography/video sourcing (his own real
  content vs. any stock) hasn't been confirmed yet — the visual-research
  phase needs to secure real Jonatas footage/photos for section 3 above;
  if none is available, that section will need to lean more on copy than
  imagery, which should be flagged then, not assumed solvable now.
- **Traffic source/volume is unconfirmed** (questionnaire.md) — this
  strategy assumes mixed organic/social plus possible local paid, which
  supports a short, low-friction page. If the client later confirms a
  traffic source with materially different intent (e.g., cold display
  ads needing more up-front context), the length call above should be
  revisited, not assumed to still hold.
